## What you may do

Nominative use — naming the project in order to refer to the project — is fine
and needs no permission:

* "works with vramfit", "built on vramfit", "compatible with vramfit";
* "a fork of vramfit", "based on vramfit", "a patched vramfit";
* writing about vramfit: tutorials, comparisons, reviews, conference talks,
  benchmarks, criticism;
* packaging vramfit for a distribution or registry under a name that makes the
  packaging relationship clear, such as `something-vramfit` or
  `vramfit-<distro>`, where the listing points back here.

The rule of thumb: say true things about the relationship, and do not let a
reader conclude that Johan Becker publishes, endorses or supports your thing.

## What you may not do

* Publish a **fork, distribution, hosted service, SaaS or commercial product
  under the name "vramfit"**, or under a name confusingly similar to it
  (`vramfitt`, `vram-fit`, `VRAMFit Pro`, `vramfit.cloud`). Fork the code freely
  — the licence lets you — but ship it under your own name.
* Use the vramfit logo, banner or visual identity as the mark of your own
  product, or in a way that suggests an official relationship.
* Register a **domain, npm package, Docker Hub namespace or organisation,
  GitHub organisation, or social media handle** that presents itself as the
  official vramfit project or as an official channel for it. This includes
  publishing to `npm` under the `vramfit` name or a scope that reads as
  official, and squatting on names in anticipation.
* Use the name in advertising, on merchandise, or in a company or product name,
  in a way that implies sponsorship, affiliation or certification.
* Modify vramfit and then distribute the result under the vramfit name in a way
  that would make Johan Becker look responsible for your changes.
